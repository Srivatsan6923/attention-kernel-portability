## Overview

If you pick the fastest attention backend on one GPU, is it still the fastest on another GPU, and what does it cost if it is not? I measured this for forward prefill and single-token KV-cache decode on six NVIDIA GPUs covering Ampere, Ada, Hopper and Blackwell, all under one software build.

Most configurations did not have a clearly fastest backend. Only 128 of 447 prefill and 409 of 1203 decode configurations had a winner that was at least 10% ahead of second place and statistically separated from it. Among those, the winner changed between GPUs in 27 of 64 prefill comparisons but only 29 of 289 decode comparisons. Across all GPU pairs, reusing the source GPU's choice usually cost almost nothing (median 1.002x prefill, 1.000x decode). The tail was worse, at 1.61x for prefill and 1.32x for decode at the 95th percentile.

### Winner and transfer cost

A configuration has a winner only if the second-fastest backend is at least 10% slower and the lower bound of a 95% bootstrap interval on that ratio is above 1. With $T_{g,c,b}$ the median latency on GPU $g$, configuration $c$ and backend $b$, and $b_1, b_2$ the fastest and second-fastest backends,

$$
R_{g,c} = \frac{T_{g,c,b_2}}{T_{g,c,b_1}}
\qquad
\operatorname{Sep}(g,c) = \mathbf{1}\!\left[R_{g,c} \ge 1.10 \;\wedge\; L_{95}(R_{g,c}) > 1\right]
$$

Failing this test means the gap was too small or too noisy to call. It does not mean the backends are equally fast.

The transfer cost is the target GPU's time for the source GPU's pick divided by the fastest time measured on the target.

$$
C_{g \rightarrow h,\,c} = \frac{T_{h,c,\,b^{*}_{g,c}}}{\min_{b \in \mathcal{B}_{h,c}} T_{h,c,b}}
$$

A value of 1.20 means the transferred choice is 20% slower than the best option on the target. When the source's pick could not run on the target, I counted the case separately instead of giving it a ratio.

## Decode

Decode does little arithmetic and reads the whole KV cache, so I report it in bytes. The KV footprint for one decode step is

$$
\text{bytes} \;=\; 2 \, B \, H_{kv} \, N \, D \, s
$$

where $B$ is batch, $H_{kv}$ the number of KV heads, $N$ the KV length, $D$ the head dimension and $s$ the bytes per element. The factor 2 counts K and V. Effective bandwidth is this byte count divided by measured time. It is a model and was not read from a profiler.

Decode was measured with and without CUDA Graphs. The figures use eager execution, and every comparison uses the same mode on both GPUs.

## Limitations

- Each GPU was measured on a different host, so hardware effects are mixed with host effects.
- The dispatch probe covered {web.headline.probed_pct.toFixed(1)}% of timed runs. For the rest, I cannot confirm which kernel actually ran.
- No profiler counters were collected, so bandwidth numbers come from the byte model.
- FlashInfer page size equals the KV length in every decode run, so the FlashInfer and FlashAttention comparison is between libraries and says nothing about paged against contiguous layouts.
- Results hold for torch {web.software.torch}, CUDA {web.software.cuda}, Triton {web.software.triton}, flash-attn {web.software.flash_attn} and FlashInfer {web.software.flashinfer}. Other versions may rank differently.
