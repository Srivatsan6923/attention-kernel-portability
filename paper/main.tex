\title{Attention Kernel Portability Across Six GPUs and Two Inference Regimes:\\

\author{Srivatsan Sarvesan \\
  University of California, San Diego \\
  \texttt{ssarvesan@ucsd.edu} \\}

\begin{abstract}
Published attention benchmarks report a winner measured on one GPU. Does that
winner survive a change of device? We time 10 prefill and 6 decode
implementations over 403 configurations on six NVIDIA GPUs spanning sm80, sm86,
sm89, sm90 and sm120, under one software distribution: torch 2.9.0+cu128, CUDA
12.8, triton 3.5.0, flash-attn 2.8.3, flashinfer 0.6.18. Restricted to the roughly one third of configurations whose fastest
implementation is separated from the runner-up on both devices, decode rankings
largely transfer across Ampere, Ada and Hopper (26 of 274 paired comparisons
flip) while prefill rankings do not (82 of 144 flip). The decode agreement then
collapses against the Blackwell part, at 64 of 84, and availability rather than
architecture accounts for 62 of those 64: FlashInfer, which wins 131 of 244
decode configurations on H100, produced no timing at all on the RTX 5090,
raising a compute-capability \code{RuntimeError} on 950 of 960 attempted rows. A
cuobjdump audit of the wheels shows the same asymmetry statically: flash-attn
2.8.3 ships no sm\_86 or sm\_89 cubins and no PTX, so on those parts it runs
sm\_80 code. The unit of performance is not (kernel, GPU) but (kernel, GPU,
regime, software-distribution snapshot).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A practitioner choosing an attention kernel reads a benchmark run on one GPU.
Does its winner survive a change of device?

We time 10 prefill and 6 decode implementations over 403 configurations on the
six GPUs of Table~\ref{tab:devices}; 73{,}230 measured rows collapse to
11{,}858 usable (GPU, configuration, implementation) medians.\footnote{Harness, analysis and figure code: \url{https://github.com/Srivatsan6923/attention-kernel-portability}}

\section{Background and related work}

Each attention paper names one device. FlashAttention
\citep{dao2022flashattention} keeps the $N \times N$ scores out of HBM, on A100;
FlashAttention-2 \citep{dao2023flashattention2} reaches 50--73\% of peak FLOPs/s,
again on A100, deferring Hopper; FlashAttention-3
\citep{shah2024flashattention3} is that deferred work, on H100 only, at 840 BF16
TFLOPs/s. Flash-Decoding \citep{dao2023flashdecoding} adds split-KV for
$q_{\text{len}} = 1$ on A100, and FlashInfer \citep{ye2025flashinfer} generalises
it into a serving engine, on A100 and H100. The generation-spanning reading is
the reader's addition, not the authors'.

Evaluation inherits the habit. The PyTorch 2 paper \citep{ansel2024pytorch2}
reports 2.27$\times$ inference geometric-mean speedup over 180+ models on one
A100 for TorchInductor, which generates Triton \citep{tillet2019triton};
KernelBench \citep{ouyang2025kernelbench} profiles 250 PyTorch workloads on an
L40S; TritonBench \citep{li2025tritonbench} evaluates 184 Triton operators on the
A100. Serving systems fix the kernel by preference order rather than by
measurement: vLLM \citep{kwon2023vllm}, Sarathi-Serve
\citep{agrawal2024sarathi} and DistServe \citep{zhong2024distserve} on A100,
Splitwise \citep{patel2024splitwise} on DGX-A100 and DGX-H100. TVM
\citep{chen2018tvm} and Ansor \citep{zheng2020ansor} instead search per target,
conceding that the right implementation is a property of the target. Outside ML
the question is mature: \citet{williams2009roofline} give the per-architecture
ceiling, and \citet{pennycook2016metric} and \citet{deakin2019portability}
define performance portability over a stated platform set and apply it across
twelve platforms.

\section{Study design}

\paragraph{Implementations and grid.} The prefill set is a naive reference, a
\code{torch.compile} path in three variants, the three PyTorch SDPA backends
(memory-efficient, flash, cuDNN), a hand-written Triton kernel, FlashAttention 2
and 3. The decode set is a naive KV-cache loop, an Inductor path,
SDPA, \code{flash\_attn\_with\_kvcache}, FlashInfer, and FlashAttention at query
length 1. A \emph{cell} keys regime, batch, query and KV head counts, head
dimension, sequence length, dtype, forward or forward-plus-backward, launch
mode, cache state and causality; the union grid is 159 prefill and 244 decode
cells, with per-device coverage in Table~\ref{tab:devices}.

\paragraph{Timing protocol.} Implementations are interleaved at the repetition
level inside one process, so a thermal or clock excursion lands on all of
them, not on whichever ran last. Latency is a CUDA-event median over inner
repetitions, then a median over process medians; confidence intervals come from
a 2000-draw cluster bootstrap over process medians, not over individual
repetitions, which share clocks and thermal point. The L2 cache
is flushed where the cell asks for a cold cache. Between-process CV is a median
0.011 on A10 (n=1871) and 0.026 on H100 (n=2474). Processes are pinned
to the NUMA node local to the GPU: two runs of the H100 decode grid at one
commit, differing only in pinning and restricted to the eager warm cells, give a
median between-process CV of 0.1116 unpinned against 0.0341 pinned over 966
(cell, implementation) triples spanning 192 cells on each side; the share of
those triples with a CV above 25\% falls from 30.7\% to 9.9\%. Both are timed by
the \code{block\_bench} wall-clock timer, not by CUDA events, so neither number
is comparable with the 0.026 above.

\paragraph{Correctness gate.} Every implementation is checked against a
reference in its equivalence class, and only rows that pass enter a ranking. Of 73{,}230 rows, 54{,}213 (74.0\%) ran and passed;
16{,}326 are \code{UNSUPPORTED}, 1{,}590 were refused in advance by an
out-of-memory predictor, and 950 raised a run-time error, all of them one
implementation on one device (Section~\ref{sec:mech}). The remaining 151 hit the
allocator despite passing the predictor, a defect in the predictor and not in
the implementation it was meant to protect. No row failed numerically. All
claims are within-device orderings compared across devices, never that device A
is faster than device B, and bandwidth denominators are measured on each host,
not taken from a datasheet.

\section{Does the benchmark measure what it claims?}

A latency is evidence about a kernel only if that kernel is the one that ran, so
we record the launched kernel names for every timed row and compare them with
the implementation the harness asked for. Of the 41{,}865 passing rows, 25{,}541
(66.5\%) captured a readable trace. Grouping by the tuple that determines
dispatch, (implementation, GPU, head dimension, dtype, GQA ratio, mode, launch),
gives 651 dispatch classes, 625 of them (96.0\%) with at least one probed row.
Among probed rows the requested kernel family is absent from the trace in 162
cases, a mismatch rate of 0.449\%, or 0.299\% over every audited row. The
unprobed 33.5\% count as unknown, never as agreement. Mismatches concentrate:
66 of the 162 are the SDPA memory-efficient backend on H100. The cells behind
the headline comparisons are probed at the dataset's own rate: of the 551
device-cells entering the restricted columns of Table~\ref{tab:flips}, 63.5\% of
passing rows carry a trace, and 51 of the 162 mismatches land on one of them.

The Inductor result needs its exact scope. Across 17{,}537 Inductor rows,
3{,}645 captured TorchInductor's \code{fuse\_attention} counter, and on all
3{,}645, on all six devices, that counter is 0. Inductor's own attention-fusion
pattern did not fire on any row whose counter we could read. The stronger claim,
that no Inductor row reached a flash kernel at all, does not hold: 45 probed
Inductor rows, all on H100, over 6 distinct cells, do carry a flash kernel in
their trace.

\section{Portability}
\label{sec:port}

\paragraph{Most cells have no decisive winner.} A winner counts as
\emph{separated} only if its ratio to the
runner-up is at least 1.10 and the cluster-bootstrap CI on that ratio excludes
1. By that criterion only 31.9\% of the 2{,}082 (device, cell) pairs with at
least two usable implementations have a separated winner, 33.4\% in decode and
29.8\% in prefill, and Figure~\ref{fig:winnermap} hatches every band where they
are a minority. A flip between implementations within a few percent of each
other is a coin toss reported as a result, so every rate below is computed
twice, over all shared cells and over cells separated on both devices.

\paragraph{The regime split.} Table~\ref{tab:flips} pools the ten device pairs
among A10, A100, L40, L40S and H100. Restriction pulls the two regimes apart. Prefill
barely moves, 82 of 144 comparisons still flip, so the disagreement survives the
exclusion of ties, while decode drops to 26 of 274, consistent with most of the
apparent decode disagreement being ties resolved differently on each device.

\paragraph{The Blackwell collapse.} The lower half of Table~\ref{tab:flips}
repeats the calculation for the five pairs including the RTX 5090. The decode
rate does not
fall under restriction, staying at 64 of 84, while prefill falls to 21 of 55.
What makes the 5090 disagree is therefore specific to decode, and
Section~\ref{sec:mech} identifies it as availability, not architecture: in 62 of
those 64 flips the winner on the other device is FlashInfer, which produced no
timing on the 5090.

\section{Mechanism: which binaries shipped}
\label{sec:mech}

\paragraph{The decode leader does not run on Blackwell.} FlashInfer
is the decode winner on 128 of 191 cells on A10, 133 of 192 on A100, 131 of 244
on H100 and 87 of 192 on L40, though only 49 of 192 on L40S, where the
SDPA path leads instead. On the RTX 5090 it wins nothing because it never
ran: of 960 attempted rows, 950 raised \code{RuntimeError: FlashInfer requires
GPUs with sm75 or higher} across 190 distinct cells, and the other 10 hit the
allocator. The text names sm75 on an sm120 part, so it is not a diagnosis, and
how much it would have won by there cannot be measured. What can be measured is
the fallback: the RTX 5090's decode winner is
\code{flash\_attn\_with\_kvcache} on 137 of 192 cells at a median 115.7 \us{}.

\paragraph{The wheels themselves.} Table~\ref{tab:prov} is a coverage map, not a
performance result. Its first two rows are the packaging asymmetry the prefill
flip rates sit beside:
FlashAttention 2 is the prefill winner on 43 of 144 A10 cells, 55 of 108 A100
cells and 72 of 144 RTX 5090 cells, and on H100 it wins none of the 159 while
FlashAttention 3 takes 81. That pattern is \emph{consistent with} the packaging
but not shown to be caused by it: we did not rebuild flash-attn for the missing
targets. FlashInfer is the other kind of gap: it JITs, so
its coverage belongs to the toolkit on the host, not to the wheel. We report its
sm\_120 failure and not a cause: every other library in the audit carries native
sm\_120 SASS built under the same CUDA 12.8, so a blanket claim that the toolkit
cannot target sm\_120 is contradicted by the audit's own rows.

\section{Threats to validity}

\paragraph{The decode comparison is confounded.} We recorded the A10 and A100 decode
rows at one commit of the harness and the L40, H100 and RTX 5090 decode rows at
a later one, days afterwards. The L40 rows carry the same manifest driver as the
A10 and A100 rows, 595.71.05; the H100 rows (580.126.09) and the RTX 5090 rows
(580.173.02) do not. Four
of the six Ampere/Ada/Hopper decode pairs cross that boundary, so for them
architecture and software version move together and the agreement cannot be
attributed to hardware alone. The confound runs against the finding rather than
for it, since the pooled decode set agrees on 248 of 274 separated comparisons
while carrying a commit and driver change on top of an architecture change. The
two pairs that stay inside one commit are A10 against A100, flipping on 2 of 32,
and H100 against L40, flipping on 9 of 33. Prefill is clean in that sense: all six devices ran prefill at one commit.
A100 prefill was however re-measured on a rented host, driver 580.126.16,
after the cluster run reached only one usable process repeat, so that device's
two regimes come from two hosts. No comparison crosses the boundary, since
every ranking is formed within one device and one regime, and the two hosts
measured copy bandwidth within 0.05\% of each other.

\paragraph{Thin repeats, and an expensive restriction.} L40 has 3 designed
process repeats against 5 on every other device, and a realised median of 2,
though its cell coverage is complete. Across the dataset 1 of 11{,}858 usable
triples has a single process repeat, and for it the bootstrap has nothing to
resample, so its CI width of 0 is an artefact. Requiring a separated winner on
both sides then discards about two thirds of the cells as ties, which is the
right call for a ranking claim but leaves the headline comparisons resting on
tens to low hundreds of paired cells.

\paragraph{Unprobed dispatch cells.} 33.5\% of passing rows captured no kernel
trace and 26 of 651 dispatch classes have no probed row at all, so the 0.449\%
mismatch rate is a rate among probed rows. On the unprobed rows the answer is
unknown, not zero.

\paragraph{Two floors under the measurement.} On L40 the median ratio of
effective KV traffic to that host's measured peak copy bandwidth is 1.038, so
the peak probe under-reads what the decode kernels sustain there. No L40 number
should be read as a fraction of achievable bandwidth: the effective GB/s
stands, the ratio does not. On H100 the CUDA-event overhead
is 5.088 \us{} against a fastest usable cell of 6.34 \us{}, with 153 of its
2{,}474 triples within 5$\times$ of the timer, so at the small-batch,
short-context, CUDA-graph end of the decode grid a winner margin competes with
timer resolution.

\paragraph{What was not measured.} Every cloud host we rented blocked
Nsight Compute and Nsight Systems, so no occupancy, cache-hit-rate or
counter-derived roofline claim appears here, and every mechanism in
Section~\ref{sec:mech} rests on a static binary audit and on run-time status
codes. We measured exactly one torch, flash-attn and flashinfer combination, so a
different wheel is a different experiment rather than a replication. What is
timed is a single attention call on six devices from one vendor.

\section{Conclusion}

On these six devices, which attention implementation is fastest is not a
property of the implementation, and a benchmark naming none of the device, the
regime and the distribution has under-specified its result.
